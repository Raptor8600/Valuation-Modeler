Valuation Modeler

DCF, Reverse DCF and IRR you can check line by line. Enter a ticker, see the inputs pulled from the company's latest SEC filings, type your own overrides, and get the same results as the Excel workbooks, with the formula, your numbers, the source and a passing test behind every figure.

Educational tool, not investment advice. It turns assumptions into numbers; it does not tell you whether to buy or sell anything.

Use it

Open the hosted app (link in the repository description). Choose Reference snapshot (offline, DDOG) or Fetch live in the sidebar and pick GAAP or Non-GAAP: the basis is shown on every tab.

Verify it

CI runs the public test suite on every push. The owner regenerates the full report, including Excel parity, with python -m scripts.verify (needs the private builder and LibreOffice).

Data and its limits

  • Financial statements: SEC EDGAR XBRL companyfacts (official filings, linked line by line). The spec this project started from suggested yfinance for statements; SEC XBRL is used instead because it is what the workbooks use (so the app and the spreadsheet cannot disagree) and every figure links to the filing it came from.
  • Market data: Yahoo Finance via yfinance (price, beta, 10-year Treasury, consensus revenue growth), cached for 15 minutes in the app. Unofficial and third-party: it can be delayed, missing or different from the exchange, and no filing can confirm it. Its source tag links to the Yahoo page.
  • GAAP figures come straight from the filings; the Non-GAAP basis is a transparent approximation (SBC, acquired-intangible amortization and an "other" line you type from the 8-K), each part switchable.
  • More in docs/limitations.md and docs/known_issues.md.

Why each default, and what would change it

Marginal tax rate

  • Default: 21% (US federal statutory)
  • Why: Statutory rate on NOPAT; effective tax rates are distorted by stock-comp deductions and NOLs. The TTM effective rate is shown beside it for reference.
  • What would change it: State taxes (often +2-5pp), large foreign operations, or a long NOL shield.

Equity risk premium

  • Default: 5.5%
  • Why: Middle of the 4-6% range of published implied ERP estimates (e.g. Damodaran).
  • What would change it: Your house view of market risk pricing; only matters in CAPM mode or through the default direct WACC.

Discount rate (WACC), one flat rate for all years

  • Default: CAPM WACC rounded to 0.1%
  • Why: A single rate is standard for a 10-year DCF; there is no term structure or changing capital mix.
  • What would change it: Your hurdle rate, a changing leverage plan, or an industry beta.

Terminal (perpetual) growth

  • Default: 2.5%
  • Why: Close to long-run nominal GDP growth; a business cannot outgrow the economy forever.
  • What would change it: Inflation expectations, a structurally declining industry (lower), or a much longer high-growth runway (extend the forecast instead).

Exit multiple (exit-multiple method only)

  • Default: 15x Year-10 EBITDA
  • Why: A mature-company multiple; today's multiple is a poor anchor for a business ten years older.
  • What would change it: Sector multiples for mature peers.

Growth fade shape

  • Default: Straight line from Year-1 growth (consensus) to terminal growth by Year 10
  • Why: Simple, transparent and conservative for high-growth companies.
  • What would change it: A visible multi-year backlog (hold growth longer: Manual Override) or a maturing market (faster fade). Sensitivity shown: Year-1 growth ±5pp.

Target operating margin

  • Default: = today's TTM margin (no operating leverage)
  • Why: Neutral starting point: the model does not assume margin expansion unless you do.
  • What would change it: Your view of margin at scale (software often 25-35%).

Capex intensity

  • Default: TTM capex ÷ TTM revenue
  • Why: Today's reinvestment rate carried forward.
  • What would change it: Planned data-centre build-outs, or a capex-light shift. Long-run capex should at least match D&A.

Working-capital intensity

  • Default: TTM residual (OCF − NI − D&A − SBC) ÷ change in revenue
  • Why: Captures the deferred-revenue tailwind of subscription businesses.
  • What would change it: Billing-term changes, or using Flat mode for businesses whose working capital scales with revenue rather than growth.

Annual change in share count

  • Default: Last year's diluted share change
  • Why: Dilution from stock compensation is a real cost per share in the value-over-time table and the IRR.
  • What would change it: A buyback programme, or a slowdown in equity grants.

Year-end discounting

  • Default: End-of-year
  • Why: Conservative and the simplest to audit; mid-year assumes cash arrives evenly through the year.
  • What would change it: House convention; mid-year is common in banking models.

Stock-based compensation treatment

  • Default: Workbook basis chosen at creation (Non-GAAP adds SBC back to operating income)
  • Why: Non-GAAP shows how the company presents itself; GAAP treats SBC as the real cost it is. Sensitivity shown: switch to GAAP.
  • What would change it: Whether you think SBC is a cash-equivalent cost (GAAP) or not. Under Non-GAAP, dilution is captured only through the share-count drift.

No tax benefit on operating losses

  • Default: No tax benefit
  • Why: Realistic unless the company has large usable NOLs or profitable group entities.
  • What would change it: Evidence of usable tax losses.

Terminal value method

  • Default: Perpetuity (Gordon growth)
  • Why: Intrinsic, does not import today's market multiples.
  • What would change it: When a transaction multiple is the relevant exit (private equity style analysis).

Methodology

This document separates the three kinds of content in the model. Every number in the app carries one of these labels.

Kind What it is Where you check it
Sourced fact A figure reported by the company (SEC XBRL) or a market feed (Yahoo Finance) The source tag: XBRL tag, period, fetch time, link to the filing
Formula Arithmetic on facts and inputs formulas.yaml / formulas.md, the Logic page, the "show your work" panels
Judgment call A default someone chose (tax rate, ERP, terminal growth, fade shape, …) assumptions.md and the Assumptions page, each with its live sensitivity

1. Data (sourced facts)

  • Financial statements: SEC EDGAR XBRL companyfacts API, parsed by valuation/sec_data.py (the same parser the Excel builder uses) so the app and the workbook read identical numbers. Trailing-twelve-month (TTM) figures = latest fiscal year + current year-to-date − prior-year year-to-date; each part is listed with its filing.
  • Filings directory: SEC submissions API (latest 10-K, 10-Q and the Item 2.02 earnings 8-K with Exhibit 99.1).
  • Market data: Yahoo Finance via yfinance (price, 5-year beta, ^TNX 10-year yield, analyst revenue-growth consensus). Third-party and unofficial; there is no filing to check these against.
  • Every live fetch is saved unmodified as a timestamped JSON file in data_snapshots/; any snapshot can be replayed.

2. From facts to inputs (formulas)

Non-GAAP bridge (SBC, acquired-intangible amortization and other adjustments, each switchable), D&A used, capex used (PP&E + capitalized software), the working-capital residual (OCF − NI − D&A − SBC), cash / debt / other claims counted (each balance-sheet line switchable), diluted share change. See data.* entries in formulas.md.

3. Discount rate

CAPM: Ke = Rf + β × ERP; Kd = interest ÷ debt if above Rf else Rf + 1.5%; WACC = We·Ke + Wd·Kd(1 − t) with market-value equity and book debt. Standard mode uses a direct WACC, defaulting to the CAPM figure rounded to 0.1%. One flat rate for all years.

4. Forecast

Ten explicit years. Automatic fade: growth falls linearly from Year-1 growth (consensus, else TTM) to terminal growth by Year 10; margin moves linearly from TTM to a target by a target year. Manual Override replaces any year; Scenario selects editable Bear / Base / Bull rows (defaults ±5pp growth fading to zero, ±3pp margin phased in).

5. DCF

UFCF_t = EBIT_t − tax + D&A − capex + ΔNWC, tax only on positive EBIT by default. Discounting end-of-year (or mid-year). Terminal value: Gordon growth on an explicitly built Year 11 (Year-10 margin, Year-11 working-capital charge), or an exit multiple on Year-10 EBITDA / UFCF / revenue. EV = Σ PV + PV(TV); equity = EV + net cash; fair value = equity ÷ diluted shares. A 7×7 sensitivity on WACC × terminal parameter, and a value-over-time path (price at the end of each year).

6. Reverse DCF

Solves the constant 10-year revenue growth g that makes model EV equal market EV (price × shares − net cash), with the same margins, tax, D&A, capex, working capital and terminal method as the DCF. Root finding: a 1% grid from −50% to +150%, then 40 bisection steps in the first bracket where the sign changes. No sign change → "No solution in range"; more than one → the lowest root is shown and flagged. A proof schedule rebuilds the years at g and must tie out to market EV.

7. IRR

Eleven multiples × trailing / forward. Metric paths come from the DCF lines; the entry multiple is computed from today's price; the exit multiple defaults to the entry multiple; exit price = multiple × metric per share (+ net cash per share for EV multiples). IRR on per-share cash flows (−price, dividends, exit price), like Excel's IRR().

8. How correctness is established

  1. Excel parity - the owner's private builder writes the workbook, LibreOffice recalculates it, and every compared value across all scenarios (DDOG GAAP / Non-GAAP, synthetic profitable and loss-making companies, every toggle, all 22 IRR multiples) is checked against the engine at 0.1% tolerance (tests/test_excel_parity.py; counts in VERIFICATION_REPORT.md).
  2. Independent reference implementation - reference_impl/simple_valuation.py (plain loops, no shared code, a different reverse-DCF formulation and a different IRR algorithm) agrees with the engine on hundreds of random cases.
  3. Live integrity checks in the app with their numbers (tie-outs, residuals, sensitivity centre, weights, units).
  4. Edge-case tests - terminal growth ≥ WACC, no-solution reverse DCF, units, zero dividends, negative / zero margins, missing data.
  5. VERIFICATION_REPORT.md - generated by one command, regenerated by CI on every push.

Limitations

  • Educational, not investment advice. The model is a structured way to state and test assumptions; its output is only as good as those assumptions.
  • Market data is third-party. Price, beta, the 10-year Treasury yield and analyst consensus come from Yahoo Finance via the unofficial yfinance library. They can be delayed, revised, missing, or differ from the exchange and from other vendors. Nothing in an SEC filing can confirm them; the source tag links to the Yahoo page so you can compare.
  • XBRL coverage. The SEC companyfacts API holds only non-dimensional facts. Segment data, multi-class share counts on the cover page and some company-specific tags are not available. A figure that is not found is left blank and flagged - never guessed - and you can type it in from the linked filing.
  • US SEC registrants only. Foreign private issuers filing 20-F/40-F are partially supported by the parser; non-SEC companies are not supported.
  • Non-GAAP is an approximation. The bridge adds back SBC, acquired-intangible amortization and an "other" figure you type from the 8-K. Companies define non-GAAP differently; compare with Exhibit 99.1.
  • Model simplifications (all visible in the Assumptions register): one flat WACC; constant D&A / capex / NWC ratios; statutory tax with no NOL schedule; no explicit SBC cash cost under non-GAAP (dilution enters only through the share-count drift); book value of debt; operating leases excluded by default.
  • Reverse DCF solves only for a constant growth rate between −50% and +150% a year; margins are not solved for.
  • IRR proxies gross profit and book value by scaling with revenue; P/E scales net income with NOPAT.
  • Thesis & Scenarios tab (probability-weighted price target) exists in the workbook but is not mirrored in the app.
  • Excel parity is tested with LibreOffice, not Microsoft Excel. The two agree on all functions used here except one documented IRR convergence case (see known_issues.md).

Engine vs spreadsheet disagreements (all of them)

Where Engine Spreadsheet (LibreOffice) Why
